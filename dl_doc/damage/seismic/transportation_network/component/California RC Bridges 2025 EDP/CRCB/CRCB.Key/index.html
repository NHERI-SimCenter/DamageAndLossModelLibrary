CRCB.Key - Shear key

The following models are available:

CRCB.Key.MultiSpan.M17 | Shear key fragility in terms of peak shear key deformation for multi-span bridges of any era.

DesignSafe PRJ-5910 v1, EDP_Fragility Database.csv, data row 18 (spreadsheet row 20), Key, EDP “Displacement (in.)”; source reference “All six listed in Table 3 of the paper”.
The model gives the probability of reaching or exceeding each damage state as a lognormal function of the peak response of the component. The demand comes from a structural analysis of the bridge. The model uses the median capacity of the source and the dispersion of 0.35 that the database assigns to every EDP-based model. Each capacity applies to one unit of the component, and the demand is the response of that unit. The demand is directional (Demand-Directional 1), so it is supplied in the direction in which the EDP is defined, as a demand of type KEY at the location and direction of the component. These models and the Sa(1.0 s)-based models of seismic/transportation_network/component/California RC Bridges 2025 and seismic/transportation_network/portfolio/California RC Bridges 2025 are alternative representations of the same damage; apply only one of them to a given bridge. The EDP definition follows Mangalathu (2017) Sec. 6.1 and Table 6.1.
The slight capacity of 1 in. is the value published in Mangalathu (2017) Table 6.13; Ramanathan (2012) Table 5.16 gives 1.5 in. Both give 5 in. for the moderate state. The values are stored as published. The database assigns these capacities to all six multi-span studies listed in Chen et al. (2025, Table 3).

Suggested Block Size: 1 EA (round up to integer quantity)


Mangalathu, S. 2017. Performance Based Grouping and Fragility Analysis of Box-Girder Bridges in California. PhD thesis, Georgia Institute of Technology, Atlanta, GA. https://hdl.handle.net/1853/58291.
Chen, S., Y. Xie, C. Wu, H. V. Burton, J. E. Padgett, and Á. Zsarnóczay. 2025. Second-generation component and system-level seismic fragility models for reinforced concrete bridges in California. Earthquake Spectra, 41 (4): 3234–3253. https://doi.org/10.1177/87552930251343634.
