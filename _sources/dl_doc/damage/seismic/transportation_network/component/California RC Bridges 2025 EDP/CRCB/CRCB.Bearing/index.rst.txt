The following models are available:


.. raw:: html

   <p class="dl_comp_name"><b>CRCB.Bearing.MultiSpan.M17</b> | Bearing fragility in terms of peak bearing deformation for multi-span bridges of any era.</p>
   <div>

| DesignSafe PRJ-5910 v1, EDP_Fragility Database.csv, data row 13 (spreadsheet row 15), Bearing, EDP "Displacement (in.)"; source reference "All six listed in Table 3 of the paper".
| The model gives the probability of reaching or exceeding each damage state as a lognormal function of the peak response of the component. The demand comes from a structural analysis of the bridge. The model uses the median capacity of the source and the dispersion of 0.35 that the database assigns to every EDP-based model. Each capacity applies to one unit of the component, and the demand is the response of that unit. The demand is directional (Demand-Directional 1), so it is supplied in the direction in which the EDP is defined, as a demand of type BRD at the location and direction of the component. These models and the Sa(1.0 s)-based models of seismic/transportation_network/component/California RC Bridges 2025 and seismic/transportation_network/portfolio/California RC Bridges 2025 are alternative representations of the same damage; apply only one of them to a given bridge. The EDP definition follows Mangalathu (2017) Sec. 6.1 and Table 6.1.
| The capacities are those of Ramanathan (2012) Tables 5.16 and 5.17 as adopted by Mangalathu (2017) Table 6.13. Mangalathu, Soleimani, and Jeon (2017, Table 4) give a moderate bearing capacity of 76 mm (3 in.) for the Era 1 bridges of that study, where this set has 4 in. The database assigns these capacities to all six multi-span studies listed in Chen et al. (2025, Table 3).


Suggested Block Size: 1 EA (round up to integer quantity)

   <p class="dl_comp_name"><b>CRCB.Bearing.SingleSpan.CPR22</b> | Bearing fragility in terms of peak bearing deformation for single-span bridges of any era (group E123-S1-C0-S).</p>
   <div>

| DesignSafe PRJ-5910 v1, EDP_Fragility Database.csv, data row 14 (spreadsheet row 16), Bearing, EDP "Displacement (in.)"; source note "For E123-S1-C0-S bridge group"; source reference "Cavalcante et al. (2022)".
| The model gives the probability of reaching or exceeding each damage state as a lognormal function of the peak response of the component. The demand comes from a structural analysis of the bridge. The model uses the median capacity of the source and the dispersion of 0.35 that the database assigns to every EDP-based model. Each capacity applies to one unit of the component, and the demand is the response of that unit. The demand is directional (Demand-Directional 1), so it is supplied in the direction in which the EDP is defined, as a demand of type BRD at the location and direction of the component. These models and the Sa(1.0 s)-based models of seismic/transportation_network/component/California RC Bridges 2025 and seismic/transportation_network/portfolio/California RC Bridges 2025 are alternative representations of the same damage; apply only one of them to a given bridge. The EDP definition follows Cavalcante et al. (2022).
| The capacities are those of Cavalcante et al. (2022, Table 8), 25 and 76 mm (1 and 3 in.), for the elastomeric bearings of single-span bridges with seat abutments in northeastern Brazil, which Chen et al. (2025) use as a proxy for California single-span bridges.
