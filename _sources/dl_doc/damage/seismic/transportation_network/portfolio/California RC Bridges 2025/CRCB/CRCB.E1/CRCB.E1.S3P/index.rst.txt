   <p class="dl_comp_name"><b>CRCB.E1.S3P.C1.D.System.BoxGirder.Circ.M17</b> | Bridge system fragility for an Era 1 (pre-1970) bridge with three or more spans, single-column bents, and diaphragm abutments. Model attributes: box-girder deck, circular columns.</p>
   <div>

| DesignSafe PRJ-5910 v1, Sa_1_Fragility Database.csv, data row 250 (spreadsheet row 252), group E1-S3P-C1-D, System; source note "Box-girder, circular column". The curve is a series-system fragility that the source study derived from correlated component demands. This curve and the component curves of the same group (seismic/transportation_network/component/California RC Bridges 2025) are two representations of the same bridge. Apply one or the other to a given bridge, because applying both to the same bridge double-counts its damage; different bridges in one regional assessment may use different representations.
| Source study: Mangalathu (2017), a doctoral thesis that groups California box-girder bridges into classes by design era, span count, bent type, abutment type, and column shape, and derives component and system fragilities for each class from nonlinear time-history analyses. The fragility analyses use 320 scaled motions from 228 records of a Caltrans NGA-West2 suite listed in thesis Appendix A. Sa(1.0 s) is the RotD50 spectral acceleration at 5% damping: for 317 of the 320 motions, the Appendix A value equals the NGA-West2 RotD50 value times the tabulated scale factor.

Model attributes: box-girder deck, five modeled spans, multiple frames, circular columns.</p>
   <div>

| DesignSafe PRJ-5910 v1, Sa_1_Fragility Database.csv, data row 252 (spreadsheet row 254), group E1-S3P-C1-D, System; source note "Box-girder, multiple frames, circular column". The curve is a series-system fragility that the source study derived from correlated component demands. This curve and the component curves of the same group (seismic/transportation_network/component/California RC Bridges 2025) are two representations of the same bridge. Apply one or the other to a given bridge, because applying both to the same bridge double-counts its damage; different bridges in one regional assessment may use different representations.
| Source study: Mangalathu, Choi, Park, and Jeon (2018), a study of tall, horizontally curved concrete box-girder bridges in California. The analyses use the Baker et al. (2011) PEER transportation suite scaled by a factor of two (320 motions). The study models one configuration: two frames, five spans, single-column bents with circular columns, an in-span hinge, and integral abutments, with three column heights and several deck curvatures. The database does not state which column height and curvature its curves represent. Chen et al. (2025, Table 3) list the study as seat-type; its bridges have integral abutments (Fig. 3 and Conclusions of the study), which is consistent with the placement of its curves in the diaphragm groups. Curves marked Disc (in-span hinge) describe the bearings and the seat at the hinge and apply only to bridges with an intermediate expansion joint. The horizontal-component definition and the spectral damping of Sa(1.0 s) are not stated.

Apply one or the other to a given bridge, because applying both to the same bridge double-counts its damage; different bridges in one regional assessment may use different representations.
| Source study: Soleimani (2017), a doctoral thesis that develops modification factors for irregular California box-girder bridges. This curve is the regular base model of the unbalanced-frame study of the thesis (Tables 7.10 and I.5). The author states in the Acknowledgements that the thesis curves "are illustrative and should not be used for deployment in ShakeCast, or other risk analysis software". This library distributes models and is not a risk-analysis application, but its models are meant for damage and loss assessments such as pelicun's, which is the kind of use the author cautions against. Users must judge for themselves whether these curves suit their use before deploying them. The ground-motion suite behind this curve is ambiguous: the thesis points both to the 320-motion Caltrans list of its Appendix F and to the 160-motion suite of Baker et al. (2011) used in its Sec. 5.1.3. The horizontal-component definition and the spectral damping of Sa(1.0 s) are not stated.
